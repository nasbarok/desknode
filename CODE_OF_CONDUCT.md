# Code of conduct

This is an **adapted** version of the Contributor Covenant 2.1. If you know the original, read
[the list of changes](#changes-made-in-this-adaptation) at the end first: the parts about
reporting and enforcement do **not** say what the original says, because the original assumes a
private channel to the people who enforce it, and this repository has none for conduct reports.
(Security vulnerabilities are different: they do reach the maintainer privately — see
[SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).)

## Our pledge

We as members, contributors, and leaders pledge to make participation in our community a
harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible
disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience,
education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, color, religion,
or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive,
and healthy community.

## Our standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment for our community include:

* Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people
* Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences
* Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback
* Accepting responsibility and apologizing to those affected by our mistakes, and learning from
  the experience
* Focusing on what is best not just for us as individuals, but for the overall community

Examples of unacceptable behavior include:

* The use of sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind
* Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks
* Public or private harassment
* Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email address, without their
  explicit permission
* Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting

## Enforcement responsibilities

In this repository, the community leaders are **the maintainer**, who owns the repository.

The maintainer is responsible for clarifying and enforcing our standards of acceptable behavior,
and may take appropriate and fair corrective action in response to any behavior they deem
inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

The maintainer has the right and responsibility to remove, edit, or reject comments, commits,
code, issues, and other contributions that are not aligned to this code of conduct,
and gives the reasons for moderation decisions when appropriate.

## Scope

This code of conduct applies within all community spaces of this project — its issues, pull
requests, commits and security advisories — and also applies when an individual is
officially representing the community in public spaces, for example when speaking in the name of
the project at an online or offline event.

## Reporting

**There is no private channel to the maintainer for reporting conduct.** This section says so
rather than pretending otherwise. (A security vulnerability is not a conduct report: it has a
private route, described in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).)

GitHub offers a way to report content privately to the maintainers of a repository — it is called
*reported content* — but only for public repositories **owned by an organization**. This
repository is owned by a **personal account**, so that option does not exist here: not today, and
not after the repository becomes public. There is no e-mail address either, deliberately: this
project publishes none.

What you can do:

* **For harassment, threats, or anything serious, report it to GitHub.** On an issue, a pull
  request or a comment, open the menu in its upper-right corner and click **Report content**, then
  **Report abuse to GitHub Support** if that choice is offered. On a person's profile, click
  **Block or Report**, then **Report abuse**. That report is read by **GitHub Support, not by the
  maintainer**.
* **To protect yourself right away, block the person** from your own account. Once blocked, they
  can no longer send you notifications — mentions included — or comment on the issues and pull
  requests you created.
* **For anything else, you can raise it in a comment.** Be aware that a comment is public.

## Enforcement

What the maintainer can do is **what GitHub gives the owner of a personal repository**, and this
section promises nothing beyond it:

* **hide** a comment — it is minimized, and anyone who can read the repository can still expand it;
* **edit** a comment to remove what breaks this code — anyone who can read the repository can
  still see the edit history;
* **delete** a comment;
* **lock** a conversation on an issue, a pull request or a commit, permanently or temporarily;
* **block** a person: in repositories the maintainer owns, a blocked person can no longer open
  issues, send pull requests, or comment;
* **limit interactions** on the repository for 24 hours, 3 days, 1 week, 1 month or 6 months.
  ⚠️ A limit applies to a whole **category** of users (new accounts, people who have not
  contributed before, or everyone who is not a collaborator), never to a single person, and it
  exists on public repositories only.

No conduct report is reviewed in private, because no conduct report can arrive in private. No
response time is promised for anything raised in public.

The maintainer respects the privacy and security of anyone who raises an incident, as far as a
public channel allows — which is less than a private one would, and that is why it is written
here.

## Enforcement guidelines

The maintainer uses these community impact guidelines to decide the consequences of any action
they deem in violation of this code of conduct:

### 1. Correction

**Community impact**: Use of inappropriate language or other behavior deemed unprofessional or
unwelcome in the community.

**Consequence**: A comment from the maintainer, in public, explaining the nature of the violation
and why the behavior was inappropriate. The offending comment may be hidden or edited. A public
apology may be requested.

### 2. Warning

**Community impact**: A violation through a single incident or series of actions.

**Consequence**: A public warning with consequences for continued behavior, and a request to stop
interacting with the people involved. The conversation may be locked. Violating these terms may
lead to a temporary restriction or a permanent ban.

### 3. Temporary restriction

**Community impact**: A serious violation of community standards, including sustained
inappropriate behavior.

**Consequence**: The conversation is locked, and interactions with the repository may be limited
for one of the durations listed under *Enforcement*. ⚠️ This is not a temporary ban of one person:
GitHub offers no such thing for a personal repository, and a limit restricts a whole category of
users. Violating these terms may lead to a permanent ban.

### 4. Permanent ban

**Community impact**: Demonstrating a pattern of violation of community standards, including
sustained inappropriate behavior, harassment of an individual, or aggression toward or
disparagement of classes of individuals.

**Consequence**: The person is blocked from the maintainer's account, so that in repositories the
maintainer owns they can no longer open issues, send pull requests or comment. The case may also
be reported to GitHub Support.

### Changes made in this adaptation

1. **The contact for reports.** The original leaves a blank for the enforcers' contact method.
   There is no private channel here for conduct reports, so the blank is not filled: the
   *Reporting* section says so, explains why, and names what GitHub actually offers.
2. **The commitment on how complaints are handled.** The original commits the enforcers to
   review and investigate every complaint both quickly **and fairly**. The whole sentence is
   removed, fairness included: nothing in this project comes with a response time, and no
   investigation of a complaint is promised.
3. **The first level of the ladder.** The original's consequence is a warning sent privately. No
   private channel exists for conduct, so the warning is a public comment.
4. **The periods of time.** The original speaks of bans and restrictions "for a specified period
   of time". They are replaced by the only durations GitHub offers a personal repository — the
   interaction limits — with the fact that they apply to a category of users, not to a person.
5. **The permanent ban** is expressed as what it is on GitHub: blocking the person.
6. **The scope.** The original's examples of representing the community through an official
   project address or social media account are removed — this project publishes no address.
   The scope names issues, pull requests, commits and security advisories. Discussions and a
   wiki are **not** named, and "wiki edits" is removed from *Enforcement responsibilities*: this
   repository has neither (measured on 2026-09-13).
7. **Who enforces.** "Community leaders" is defined as the maintainer, "will take corrective
   action" becomes "may take corrective action", and "will communicate reasons for moderation
   decisions" becomes "gives the reasons for moderation decisions".
8. **The privacy of the reporter.** The original obligates the enforcers to respect it. Here it is
   respected as far as a public channel allows, and the limit is written.
9. **Layout.** A *Reporting* section is split out of *Enforcement*; headings use sentence case;
   reference-style links are rewritten as inline links; the address of version 2.1 is the one the
   site serves today (the original's address ending in `.html` redirects to it — measured on
   2026-09-13); an introduction pointing to this list is added at the top.
10. **The second level of the ladder.** The original's warning is not said to be public; here it
   is, since no private channel exists for conduct. The original's condition — no interaction with the people
   involved for a specified period — becomes a **request** to stop interacting: nothing here can
   enforce it for a period. The original's "including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing
   the Code of Conduct" and "external channels like social media" are removed with it.
11. **The third level of the ladder** is renamed from "Temporary Ban" to "Temporary
   restriction", and level 2 now leads to "a temporary restriction or a permanent ban". The original's sentence forbidding any public or private
   interaction with the people involved during the ban is removed: a limit on the repository
   cannot enforce it, and nothing outside the repository can either.
12. **Consequences that GitHub offers and the original does not name** are added: hiding or
   editing the offending comment (level 1), locking the conversation (levels 2 and 3), and
   reporting the case to GitHub Support (level 4). The *Enforcement* section lists the moderation
   tools a personal repository has, and the *Reporting* section adds blocking as a way for
   anyone to protect themselves.
13. **The introduction of the ladder.** "Community leaders will follow these Community Impact
   Guidelines" becomes "The maintainer uses these community impact guidelines".
14. **Conduct reports and security reports are told apart.** The statements that no private
   channel exists are limited to conduct, and point to the security policy, which has one.
